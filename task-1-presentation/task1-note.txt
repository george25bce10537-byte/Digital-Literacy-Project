For Task 1, I decided to create a Digital Literacy Awareness infographic using Canva. Honestly, the choice felt pretty straightforward — Canva is beginner-friendly, the designs look polished, and there are plenty of free templates that work well for educational content. My goal was simple: help students actually understand what digital literacy means and why it matters in their day-to-day lives, both academically and personally.
I organized the infographic around five key areas — what digital literacy really means, helpful digital tools for students, staying safe online, building a professional presence, and email etiquette. I picked these topics because, let's be real, college students are constantly on digital platforms — for classes, communication, job applications — and most of us never really stop to think about whether we're using them well.
The part I enjoyed most was figuring out how to make the information visually appealing without dumbing it down. But it wasn't without its challenges — the trickiest part was knowing when to stop. There's so much worth saying about digital literacy that keeping the layout clean and uncluttered took more thought than I expected.
Looking back, this task shifted how I think about technology. It's not just about knowing how to use the tools — it's about using them responsibly, safely, and in a way that actually reflects well on you. That's a bigger idea than I initially gave it credit for.
